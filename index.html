Lipton TeaCrafter Kiosk UI

A step-by-step tea-customization experience that makes personal choices visible through an evolving teapot illustration.

Interaction designKiosk UIIllustration

Overview

TeaCrafter is a self-service kiosk concept for creating a personalized tea blend. I designed the experience around a 19-inch touchscreen, balancing the freedom to customize with the clarity and efficiency needed in a public setting.

Design approach

The flow moves from base tea and intensity to flavors, add-ins, packaging, review, payment, and collection. Each stage focuses on a manageable set of decisions, while an evolving teapot illustration shows the blend taking shape. Labels, checkmarks, and selection states support the color cues rather than relying on color alone.

My contribution

I developed the user flow, illustrated interface direction, screen designs, and design rationale. The review step supports editing before payment, and the final screens communicate preparation and collection. This is an ENP 166 academic design concept, not a launched Lipton product.

JENN’S IDEA / UI/UX & Product Thinking
Condé Nast concept · Product strategy & interaction design · 2026

MISE — Digital Recipe Book

A digital cooking companion developed from product concept to six core wireframes, connecting recipe discovery, adaptation, shopping, and guided cooking.

Product strategyInformation architectureWireframingInteraction design

Overview

MISE explores how Condé Nast’s culinary content could become a flexible digital cookbook for smartphones and tablets. The project connects recipe inspiration with the practical decisions involved in preparing a meal, supporting beginners and experienced home cooks.

Product concept

I defined user needs and ten potential features, then compared three models: Editorial Discovery, Digital Cookbook, and Cooking Journey. I selected the Digital Cookbook because a familiar recipe-centered structure combines curated content with optional guidance without forcing every user through the same sequence.

Interface & user flow

I developed a UI structure and six core wireframes: Discover, Search Results, Recipe Details, Adjust Recipe, Shopping, and Cook Mode. Recipe Details acts as the central connection point, allowing users to review preparation needs, adjust servings and ingredients, mark what they already have, and carry missing items into Shopping.

Key interaction decisions

Shopping and recipe adjustment remain optional. Changes can be reviewed and reversed, while Cook Mode brings the current instruction, ingredient quantities, timer, and optional tutorials together. The intended experience preserves progress and lets users control how much support they need.

My contribution & context

I developed the brief, feature exploration, concept comparison, information architecture, wireframes, and design rationale across ENP 166 Assignments 2 and 3. These are two stages of the same academic project. MISE is a design concept, not commissioned or launched Condé Nast work, and the proposed interactions are not claims of a working or user-tested product.

JENN’S IDEA / UI/UX & Product Thinking
Automated dog-wash kiosk · Individual academic project · 2026

PawPup Spa Kiosk

A touchscreen kiosk concept that guides dog owners from package selection to wash completion, keeping machine status visible and every stage under owner control.

Kiosk UIInteraction designUser flowsError recoveryPrototyping

Overview

PawPup Spa is a touchscreen kiosk concept for an automated dog-washing service. The machine performs the wash while the owner stays with their dog and decides when to continue. I explored how a friendly interface could explain an unfamiliar service, make equipment behavior predictable, and support decisions throughout the session.

From flow to prototype

I mapped the complete journey and exception paths, developed hand-drawn wireframes, and translated them into a consistent visual interface and prototype. The flow separates package selection, payment, owner preparation, machine checks, washing, and exit so that completing one step does not unexpectedly trigger the next.

Clear choices before the wash

Dog-profile inputs and package cards show the options, prices, estimated durations, and included stages before payment. Required fields and visible checkmarks help owners understand what is selected. A payment review and a separate readiness checklist make the transition from purchase to equipment use explicit.

Guidance without taking control away

During washing, stage indicators, output-status messages, and nearby instructions explain what the machine is doing and what the owner can do next. Owners can add product, repeat rinsing, select an airflow level, or skip drying. Estimated time supports planning without acting as a fixed cutoff.

Recovery is part of the experience

Pause preserves the current stage, while Emergency stop is designed to stop equipment outputs. Temperature and gate alerts explain the issue, the stopped outputs, and the required next action. Resuming requires checks and an explicit owner action. The proposed flow also includes remote support, an early exit, dog-removal guidance, and applicable refund information.

Visual direction & next steps

Cream backgrounds, a blue-green palette, orange accents, and playful headings create an approachable identity. Consistent layouts, labels, icons, and checkmarks reinforce meaning beyond color. This individual ENP 166 project includes a user-flow map, wireframes, interface screens, a prototype demonstration, and design rationale. It is a design concept rather than a deployed machine; further usability testing should examine readability, touch targets, and understanding of controls and recovery.
